How well does it work, and what is it not?

Every number on this page is measured against a dataset where we planted known illicit cases. That is the only reason precision and recall can be quoted at all — real traffic comes with no answer key. The page also states what the numbers do not cover, because a scorecard that only reports successes is advocacy rather than evidence.

Does the score mean what it says?

If the console prints 90, is it right about 90% of the time? Measured, not assumed — a forest's vote-count probabilities are usually overconfident.

Is it just hand-written rules?

The question every evaluator asks first. An argument is worth less than a measurement, so it is measured three ways.

Was the model choice justified?

A random forest was chosen over a deliberately simple linear model. If the linear model had matched it, the honest thing would have been to ship the linear model.
