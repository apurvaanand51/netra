Find the wallets worth investigating — and show the evidence.

Bitcoin transactions are public but not named. NETRA reads a bulk dump of network traffic and transaction records, works out which wallets are controlled by the same person, works out which IP addresses controlled them, and then ranks the groups that behave like money laundering — with the reason attached to each one.

Step 1 — read the traffic

Any of CSV, JSON, XML, or a SQLite database. Rows that cannot be parsed are reported with a reason, never quietly dropped.

Step 2 — what happens to it

  1. Group wallets into owners. In Bitcoin you must sign for every wallet you spend from, so wallets spent together belong to one person.
  2. Join the two worlds. We match the IP that broadcast a transaction with the wallet it moved. That is how a wallet gets a country.
  3. Describe the whole capture. Volume, value distribution, geography and behaviour composition across everything read.
  4. Rank, and explain. Two trained models score every group, and each score comes with the factors that produced it and the money trail it follows.
